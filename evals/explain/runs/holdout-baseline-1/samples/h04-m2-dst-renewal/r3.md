`kestrel-scheduler` fired `charge_renewals` twice because Europe/Berlin repeated the wall-clock time 02:30 on 25 October, and the scheduler's 60-second guard compares UTC instants. Aelita recommends that the scheduler fire once per local wall-clock minute. This is a small change in `scheduler/clock.py` and `scheduler/loop.py`.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. `kestrel-scheduler` fires `charge_renewals` at 00:30:04Z, which is 02:30+0200 in Berlin. `charge_renewals` charges 212 subscriptions and inserts `renewal_charges` rows with `settled=false`. `renews_on` stays unchanged.
2. `send_dunning` fires at 00:45:03Z and sends 41 emails.
3. At 01:00Z Berlin clocks fall back from 03:00 to 02:00. Local time 02:00 to 02:59 happens again.
4. At 01:30:02Z the local time is 02:30+0100, so `matches` accepts `30 2 * * *`. `due_jobs` sees the previous fire 3598 seconds ago. That is above `MIN_GAP_SECONDS`, so it fires the job again.
5. `charge_renewals` selects `renews_on <= today`. Only `settle_renewals` advances `renews_on`, and it has not run, so the same 212 rows match. `psp.charge` sends no idempotency key, so Payrail charges every card again.
6. `send_dunning` fires again at 01:45:01Z and sends the same 41 emails.
7. `settle_renewals` fires at 04:00:03Z. It reports `settled=424` and advances `renews_on` once per subscription. Nothing flags the duplicates.
8. 212 subscribers see two identical charges dated 25 October, as KB-519 reports.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 4.

4. At 01:30:02Z `due_jobs` computes the local wall-clock minute `2026-10-25 02:30`. It equals the stored key from step 1, so it skips `charge_renewals`.
5. No second charge happens. The 212 subscriptions are charged once.
6. `send_dunning` is skipped the same way at 01:45Z. The 41 emails go out once.
7. `settle_renewals` fires at 04:00:03Z and reports `settled=212`.
8. Each of the 212 subscribers pays once.

**What changes**

- `scheduler/loop.py`: old behaviour is `last_fired[job.name] = now_utc`. New behaviour stores the local wall-clock minute, `local.replace(second=0, microsecond=0, tzinfo=None)`.
- `scheduler/clock.py`: delete `MIN_GAP_SECONDS` and the UTC gap check. Add a check that skips a job when its current local wall-clock minute is less than or equal to the stored one. This also keeps the 15-second tick from firing twice in one minute.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends the wall-clock key, because it removes the cause of the repeat. The scheduler stays in one process and keeps its state in memory.
- **Cost:** about five changed lines.
- **What can break:** on spring-forward, 02:30 never exists, so `charge_renewals` skips that day. The next day's `renews_on <= today` query catches those subscriptions up.
- **Restart gap:** a scheduler restart inside the repeated hour loses `last_fired`, so the guard does not cover that case.
- **Minutely jobs:** a `* * * * *` job would pause for the repeated hour. No such schedule exists today.

**Alternative: idempotency key in the charge path.** `billing/renewals.py` would pass a key such as `Renewal {sub.id} {today}` through `billing/psp.py`. It beats the recommendation because it also survives restarts and manual reruns. It costs a change to the money path, and I could not verify that Payrail honours an `Idempotency-Key` header, because no file here settles it. It becomes the primary if Payrail confirms support and reruns outside DST matter more.
