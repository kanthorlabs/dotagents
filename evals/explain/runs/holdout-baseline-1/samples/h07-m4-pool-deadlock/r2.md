`checkout` holds one pool connection and then `hold_seats` asks the same pool for a second one. Once 10 requests are in flight on a replica, all 10 connections are taken and each holder waits for a connection that only another waiting holder could free. Aelita recommends passing the open `conn` into `hold_seats`.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. Normal traffic gives `in_flight=3` on `checkout-api-2`. Three requests hold at most 6 of the 10 connections in `pool`, so the second borrow always succeeds.
2. At 19:00:01 the Halden Arena on-sale puts 14 requests in flight. Ten of them take a connection at `app/checkout.py:6`, so `pool_in_use=10`.
3. Each of those 10 calls `hold_seats` and asks for a second connection at `app/seats.py:5`. The pool is empty, so they wait. The other 4 requests wait at `app/checkout.py:6`, so `pool_waiting=14`.
4. No holder can finish without its second connection, and none releases its first one. This is a deadlock inside one process. Postgres shows `active_connections=38` of 200 because the limit is `max_size=10` per replica, not the database.
5. At 19:00:31 the `timeout=30` expires. The log shows 10 `PoolTimeout` errors in `hold_seats` and 4 in `checkout`. All 14 requests return 500 together.
6. The 12 queued requests refill the pool and deadlock again. The same failure repeats at 19:01:02.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 3.

3. Each of the 10 holders runs the seat `UPDATE` on the connection it already holds. It needs no second borrow.
4. Each request commits and releases its connection within milliseconds. The 4 extra requests wait briefly at `app/checkout.py:6` and then proceed.
5. No request waits 30 seconds, so no `PoolTimeout` occurs and no 500s are returned.

**What changes**

- `app/seats.py`: `hold_seats(event_id, seat_ids)` borrows its own connection with `pool.connection()`. The new `hold_seats(conn, event_id, seat_ids)` uses the caller's `conn`, and the `pool` import is deleted.
- `app/checkout.py`: line 12 changes from `hold_seats(order.event_id, order.seat_ids)` to `hold_seats(conn, order.event_id, order.seat_ids)`.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends passing `conn` because it removes the cause. Each request then needs exactly one connection, so no concurrency level can deadlock. `hold_seats` also joins the `conn.transaction()` block. Before, it committed on its own connection, so a failed order could leave seats held for 10 minutes. Now they roll back with the order. `app/api.py` is the only caller of `checkout`, and `checkout` is the only caller of `hold_seats`, so nothing else breaks. Throughput stays capped at 10 concurrent checkouts per replica, so extra requests queue. Seat `UPDATE` row locks are now held until commit, which is the correct behaviour.

**Alternative: cap in-flight checkouts below the pool size.** A semaphore of 5 per replica in `app/api.py` would stop the deadlock without touching `hold_seats`. It beats the recommendation only if `hold_seats` must stay on a separate connection. It wastes half the pool and leaves the seat-hold leak in place.
