Aelita recommends approving the proposal with one added change. Concurrency lets an old retried reading overwrite a newer one in `latest_reading`. That table feeds the `freezer-temp-high` alert. A guard on `read_at` in `UPSERT_LATEST` closes the gap.

**Existing behaviour**

1. `deploy/consumer.yaml` sets `max_ack_pending: 1`. The consumer `ingest` handles one message at a time.
2. `with_retries` sleeps 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 seconds on `TransientError`. That is up to 62 s per message.
3. Every later reading waits. Order per device is therefore preserved by accident.
4. `UPSERT_LATEST` has no `read_at` check, so it relies on that order.

**Timeline without the guard**

1. Freezer sensor `f-7` sends reading A at 10:14:00, then reading B at 10:14:30 and reading C at 10:15:00.
2. Timescale fails over. `save_reading` raises `TransientError` for A. The handler calls `nak(delay=2s)`.
3. With `max_ack_pending: 256`, B and C are delivered while A waits. The database is back, so both save.
4. `latest_reading` for `f-7` now holds C.
5. A is redelivered at its sixth attempt, about 10:15:02. It saves.
6. `UPSERT_LATEST` overwrites C with A. `latest_reading` now holds an older value, and it stays stale until 10:15:30. If A is the last reading before `f-7` goes offline, it stays stale for good. The live map and `freezer-temp-high` read the wrong value.

**Timeline with the guard**, diverges at step 6.

6. `UPSERT_LATEST` adds `WHERE latest_reading.read_at < EXCLUDED.read_at`. A has an older `read_at` than C, so the update is skipped. `readings` still receives A. `latest_reading` keeps C.

**What changes**

- `ingest/retry.py`: delete.
- `ingest/handler.py`: remove `with_retries`. Add `nak(delay=...)` on `TransientError`. Publish to `ingest.dlq` and ack when `num_delivered` reaches 6.
- `deploy/consumer.yaml`: `max_ack_pending: 1` becomes `256`. `max_deliver: 1` becomes `6`.
- `ingest/store.py`: `UPSERT_LATEST` gains the `WHERE` guard above. This part is new.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends approving with the guard. The 26 October log supports the goal. 17 stalls at 62 s is about 17.6 minutes of blocking, and lag took 16 minutes to clear after the failover. The guard costs one comparison per upsert. The retry schedule stays the same, with six attempts and the same delays. One risk remains. Redelivery can insert a duplicate row in `readings` if an ack is lost after the commit. `readings` has no visible unique key, so reports could double count the rare duplicate.

**Alternative: keep `max_ack_pending: 1` and shorten the delays.** Ordering stays safe and nothing else changes. It still blocks every reading during a failover. It becomes the primary if you cannot change `store.py` today.
