Aelita recommends approving the proposal with one addition. `save_reading` must stop an older reading from overwriting a newer one in `latest_reading`. Without that guard, redelivery puts stale values on the live map and in the `freezer-temp-high` alert.

**Existing behaviour**

1. `deploy/consumer.yaml` sets `max_ack_pending: 1`, so `sparrow-ingest` handles one message at a time.
2. On `TransientError`, `with_retries` sleeps 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 seconds, then tries once more. That is 62 s.
3. The whole consumer waits during those 62 s. `logs/lag-2026-10-26.txt` shows 17 such stalls.
4. Because only one message is in flight, readings are saved in arrival order. `UPSERT_LATEST` in `ingest/store.py` relies on this. It has no `read_at` check.

**Timeline without the fix**

Freezer sensor `freezer-7` sends reading R1 at 10:14:05 with -20 °C. It sends R2 at 10:14:35 with -12 °C. Timescale fails over from 10:14:03 to 10:14:40.

1. R1 is delivered at 10:14:05. The save fails. The handler naks with a 2 s delay.
2. R1 fails again at 10:14:07, 10:14:11, 10:14:19 and 10:14:35. Its naks grow to 32 s. Its sixth delivery is due at 10:15:07.
3. R2 is delivered at 10:14:35 and fails. It is redelivered at 10:14:37 and fails again.
4. R2 is redelivered at 10:14:41. Timescale is back, so R2 saves. `latest_reading` for `freezer-7` is -12 °C.
5. R1 is redelivered at 10:15:07 and saves. `UPSERT_LATEST` has no `read_at` check, so it overwrites -12 °C with -20 °C.
6. `latest_reading` now holds an older, colder value. The live map is wrong, and `freezer-temp-high` has its 10-minute window reset by a value that is no longer true.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 5.

5. R1 is redelivered at 10:15:07 and runs `UPSERT_LATEST`. The guard sees that R1's `read_at` is older than the stored one, so it skips the update. `readings` still gets the R1 history row.
6. `latest_reading` stays at -12 °C. The alert and the live map stay correct.

**What changes**

- `ingest/retry.py`: delete, as Ulrich proposes.
- `ingest/handler.py`: remove `with_retries`. On `TransientError`, call `msg.nak(delay=...)`. At delivery 6, publish to `ingest.dlq` and ack.
- `deploy/consumer.yaml`: `max_ack_pending: 1` becomes `256`. `max_deliver: 1` becomes `6`.
- `ingest/store.py`: add `WHERE latest_reading.read_at < EXCLUDED.read_at` to the `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` in `UPSERT_LATEST`.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends Ulrich's change plus the `read_at` guard. It meets the goal. A failing reading no longer holds the other readings for 62 s. On 26 October the failover backlog peaked at 41,200 messages and took 16 minutes to clear. Concurrent handling should shorten that. The guard is one SQL clause. It costs nothing at normal load. The `ingest.dlq` path and the `max_deliver: 6` count are unchanged by it. The guard does not fix everything. I did not find `ingest/errors.py` or `ingest/parse.py` in the repository. So I could not check which errors count as `TransientError`.

**Alternative: one consumer per device group.** Filter subjects so each device always goes to the same consumer, with `max_ack_pending: 1` in each. Order stays per device and `store.py` needs no change. It costs more consumers to deploy and tune. It becomes the primary if `readings` must also be written in strict arrival order.
