Backoff in `sync/crm_client.py` cannot fix this. Juniper allows 100 requests per minute, and `upsert_contact` spends one request per contact. Aelita recommends switching `sync/run_sync.py` to `POST /v2/contacts:batchUpsert` and keeping a small `Retry-After` retry as a safety net.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. Marketing imports 45,600 Fairhaven leads on 26 October. `changed_contacts` returns 46,800 rows.
2. At 01:00 on 27 October, `run_sync` starts 8 workers. Each calls `upsert_contact`, which sends one `PUT` per contact.
3. The workers send about 400 requests per minute. Juniper accepts 100 and returns 429 with `Retry-After: 60` for about 300.
4. `sync_one` logs a warning for each 429 and skips `mark_synced`. Those contacts wait for the next night.
5. Only 100 contacts succeed per minute. Two hours allow at most 12,000.
6. At 02:59:58, `activeDeadlineSeconds: 7200` kills the job with 11,940 upserted and 34,860 pending.

**Timeline with your backoff**, diverges at step 4.

4. `upsert_contact` waits 60 seconds on each 429 and retries. Fewer requests are wasted.
5. Throughput stays at 100 contacts per minute. 46,800 contacts need 468 minutes, against a 120 minute deadline.
6. At the deadline, about 12,000 contacts are upserted and about 34,800 are pending. The failure is unchanged.

**Timeline with batch upsert**, diverges at step 2.

2. `run_sync` groups the contacts into 468 batches of 100. Each batch is one `batchUpsert` call.
3. Each call takes 5 to 7 seconds. 6 workers send 51 to 72 calls per minute, under the limit of 100.
4. `run_sync` reads the per-contact result and calls `mark_synced` only for contacts that succeeded.
5. 468 calls finish in about 10 minutes.
6. The 29 October 01:00 run clears the backlog before the Friday 30 October 09:00 UTC deadline in `TS-77`. Each November expo of 40,000 leads needs about 400 calls, so it also fits.

**What changes**

- `sync/crm_client.py`: add `upsert_batch(contacts)`, which posts to `/contacts:batchUpsert` and returns the per-contact results. On 429, it sleeps for `Retry-After` and retries up to 3 times. `upsert_contact` stays.
- `sync/run_sync.py`: `sync_one` becomes `sync_batch`. `main` splits `contacts` into chunks of 100. `WORKERS` goes from 8 to 6, because 8 workers at 5 seconds per call peak at 96 per minute, too close to 100.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends batch upsert, because it removes the cause. The job sends 100 times fewer requests. It costs one new client function and a change to the loop. What can break: a partial batch failure. Marking only the successful contacts handles that. The `Retry-After` retry stays as a guard for the occasional 429.

**Alternative: enterprise tier.** Juniper raises the limit to 1,000 requests per minute. It needs no code change. It costs 1,450 USD per month and has a 3 week lead time, so it misses the Friday deadline. It becomes the primary if `batchUpsert` proves unreliable.
