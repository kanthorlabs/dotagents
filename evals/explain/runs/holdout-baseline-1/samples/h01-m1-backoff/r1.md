Backoff alone will not fix the sync, because the Juniper limit caps `tidepool-sync` at about 12,000 contacts per night. Aelita recommends moving `sync/crm_client.py` to `POST /v2/contacts:batchUpsert` and keeping a small `Retry-After` retry as a safety net.

**Timeline without the fix** (backoff only)

1. The Fairhaven import adds 45,600 leads on 26 October. The 27 October run starts with 46,800 changed contacts.
2. `run_sync.py` runs 8 workers. Each calls `upsert_contact`, which sends one `PUT` per contact. The log shows about 400 requests per minute.
3. The limit is 100 requests per minute per key. About 300 per minute return 429, and `sync_one` logs a warning and skips them.
4. With backoff, the workers wait and retry, but one contact still costs one request. Throughput stays at 100 contacts per minute.
5. The job has `activeDeadlineSeconds: 7200`, which allows 12,000 contacts per night. The 27 October run upserted 11,940.
6. Three runs remain before Friday 09:00 UTC, on 28, 29 and 30 October. They give 36,000 slots against 34,860 pending plus about 3,420 normal changes. The campaign misses its deadline. The November expos, at 40,000 leads each, would need about 4 nights each.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 2.

2. `upsert_contacts` groups contacts into batches of 100. The 8 workers each send one batch call. That is 468 calls for 46,800 contacts.
3. A batch takes 5 to 7 seconds, so 8 workers send 69 to 96 calls per minute. That is under the limit, so no 429 appears.
4. A 429 that does occur is retried after the `Retry-After` delay.
5. The run finishes in about 6 to 7 minutes, far inside the 7200 s deadline.
6. All Fairhaven leads reach Juniper CRM on the 28 October run. A 40,000-lead expo takes about 6 minutes.

**What changes**

- `sync/crm_client.py`: add `upsert_contacts(batch)`, which posts to `/contacts:batchUpsert`. Add a retry that sleeps for `Retry-After` on 429, with a small attempt cap. Keep or delete `upsert_contact` as you prefer.
- `sync/run_sync.py`: `sync_one` becomes `sync_batch`. `main` splits `contacts` into chunks of 100. `mark_synced` runs only for contacts that the per-contact response reports as successful.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends batching, because it removes the root cause of one request per contact. It costs one rewrite of the call path. Two things can break it. The per-contact response format is not in `docs/juniper-api.md`, so the success parsing needs a check against a real response. At 5 s per call, 8 workers reach 96 calls per minute, which is close to the limit. If that is too tight, reduce `WORKERS` to 6.

**Alternative: enterprise tier.** The limit rises to 1,000 requests per minute. It costs 1,450 USD per month and has a 3-week lead time, so it cannot meet the 30 October deadline. It becomes the primary only if Juniper refuses batch calls for this key.
