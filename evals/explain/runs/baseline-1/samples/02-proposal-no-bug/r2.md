`fernway-web` should add `cache: pnpm` to the `actions/setup-node@v4` step in `ci.yml`. Aelita expects it to cut about 3 min 05 s from the median `ci` run, from 11 min 05 s to about 8 min 00 s. That saves about 21 hours of runner time and about 10 USD per week.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. A developer pushes a branch. `ci` starts on `ubuntu-24.04`.
2. `actions/checkout@v4`, `pnpm/action-setup@v4` and `actions/setup-node@v4` run. Nothing is cached.
3. `Install` runs `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` and downloads every package. The median is 3 min 40 s.
4. `Lint` and `Test` run. Together they take 7 min 25 s.
5. The run ends at 11 min 05 s while the developer waits to merge. Over week 43, 412 runs spent about 25 hours and 12 USD on `Install`.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 3.

3. `setup-node` restores the pnpm store from a key built from `pnpm-lock.yaml`. On a hit, the 1.1 GB store is already on disk.
4. `Install` links packages from the store. The measured median with a warm store is 35 s.
5. `Lint` and `Test` run unchanged. The run ends at about 8 min 00 s. Each of the 412 runs saves 3 min 05 s, about 1,270 minutes in total.

**What changes**

- `.github/workflows/ci.yml`: add `cache: pnpm` under `with:` in the `actions/setup-node@v4` step. It sits after `pnpm/action-setup@v4`, which `setup-node` needs in order to find the store path. Nothing is deleted.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends `cache: pnpm` because it is one line, and `setup-node` handles the key and the store path. The cost is 1.1 GB per distinct lockfile in a 10 GB repository limit. That leaves room for about 9 lockfile versions before GitHub evicts the oldest. When `pnpm-lock.yaml` changes, that run misses the cache and takes about 3 min 40 s. Later runs hit it.

**Unverified.** The 35 s figure comes from 20 manual runs with a warm store. No file shows how long a real restore of 1.1 GB takes. A trial branch with two pushes would settle it. The saving could be a little smaller than 3 min 05 s.

**Alternative: `actions/cache` with an explicit store path.** It gives control over the key and over restore keys. It costs about 8 more lines to maintain. It becomes the primary if the team needs a custom key, such as one per OS.
