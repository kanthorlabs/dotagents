`fernway-web` CI should cache the pnpm store by adding `cache: pnpm` to the `actions/setup-node@v4` step in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. By the team's own measurements, that cuts the median run from 11 min 05 s to about 8 min 00 s.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. A developer pushes a branch. Workflow `ci` starts on `ubuntu-24.04`.
2. `actions/checkout@v4`, `pnpm/action-setup@v4` and `actions/setup-node@v4` run. No cache is restored.
3. Step `Install` downloads 1.1 GB of packages. The median is 3 min 40 s.
4. `pnpm lint` and `pnpm test` run.
5. The run ends after a median of 11 min 05 s. The developer waits for it before merging.
6. Over week 43, 412 runs paid the full download. A warm store needs only 35 s, so the extra 3 min 05 s per run adds up to 1,270 runner minutes and about 10.16 USD.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 3.

3. `setup-node` restores the pnpm store from a cache keyed on the lockfile hash. `Install` takes about 35 s.
4. `pnpm lint` and `pnpm test` run unchanged.
5. The run ends after about 8 min 00 s.
6. Each run saves 3 min 05 s of developer wait. The team saves about 21 runner hours and about 10 USD per week.

**What changes**

- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` old behaviour: the `setup-node` step sets only `node-version: 22`. New behaviour: add `cache: pnpm` under `with:`. The `pnpm/action-setup` step already runs first, so no reordering is needed.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends `cache: pnpm` on `setup-node`, because it is one line and has no extra moving parts. The main gain is developer wait time. The runner bill is only about 10 USD per week. The cache has two limits:

- **Misses:** a push that changes the lockfile misses the cache. It pays the full 3 min 40 s and saves a new 1.1 GB entry.
- **Eviction:** the 10 GB repository limit holds about 9 such entries. Heavy lockfile churn can evict the entry that the default branch needs.
- **Measurement:** the 35 s figure comes from 20 manual runs with a warm store. The real hit rate is unmeasured. I found no lockfile in this directory, so I could not check the key input.

**Alternative: explicit `actions/cache` on the store path.** It gives control over the key and the fallback keys. A new branch could then restore the nearest older store after a lockfile change. It costs about 10 more lines of workflow. It becomes the primary if the hit rate turns out low.
