`nginx` rejects every Halvorsen Marine drawing because `client_max_body_size 10m` is far below their 60 to 180 MB files. Aelita recommends raising the limit to 200 MB in `nginx` and in Flask, with no frontend change.

**Existing behaviour**

1. `nginx` caps request bodies at 10 MB (`deploy/nginx/pinewood.conf:8`). It buffers the whole body to disk, then proxies to `pinewood-app`.
2. Flask caps bodies at 10 MB as well (`MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH` in `app/settings.py:3`). It is a second, hidden limit.
3. `upload_file` in `app/uploads.py` streams the file to the bucket `pinewood-files` through `storage.put`.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. Eirik Dahl uploads a 94 MB drawing through the web form. It is a plain POST to `/api/files`.
2. `nginx` reads the `Content-Length` header of 94371840 bytes. The log shows this at 09:14:02 on 27 October.
3. `nginx` compares it to `client_max_body_size 10m`.
4. `nginx` returns "413 Request Entity Too Large". The request never reaches `pinewood-app`.
5. The 167772160-byte upload at 09:31:47 fails the same way. Every Halvorsen Marine drawing fails, and the yard review on 2 November is at risk.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 3.

3. `nginx` compares the body to `client_max_body_size 200m` on `/api/files`. The body passes. `nginx` buffers it to `client_temp`.
4. `nginx` proxies the buffered body to `pinewood-app`. Flask accepts it under the new `MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH` of 200 MB.
5. `storage.put` sends 180 MB to the bucket in about 3 seconds at 60 MB per second. That is well under the gunicorn `timeout = 30`. The API returns 201.

**What changes**

- `deploy/nginx/pinewood.conf`: add a `location = /api/files` block with `client_max_body_size 200m;` and the same `proxy_pass` and headers. The other `/api/` routes keep 10m.
- `app/settings.py`: `MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH` changes from `10 * 1024 * 1024` to `200 * 1024 * 1024`.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends the two limit changes. They need no frontend release, so they meet the Friday 30 October deadline.
- **Time:** a 180 MB file takes about 72 seconds over the 20 Mbit/s uplink. `nginx` buffers the body first, so the gunicorn worker is busy for about 3 seconds, not 72. The 30-second timeout is safe.
- **Disk:** 25 files of 180 MB is at most 4.5 GB per day. `nginx` deletes each temp file after the request, so the 20 GB volume is enough.
- **Risk:** Flask's limit applies to every route, so 200 MB now applies app-wide. Only `/api/files` accepts uploads today, and `nginx` still caps the other routes at 10m. Slow clients could hold `nginx` connections longer.
