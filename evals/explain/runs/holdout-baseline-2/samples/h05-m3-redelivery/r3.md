Aelita recommends approving the proposal on one condition. `save_reading` in `ingest/store.py` must stop a late retry from overwriting a newer `latest_reading` row. Without that guard, the redelivery order lets an old reading replace a new one.

**Timeline without the fix**

Today `max_ack_pending: 1` makes `sparrow-ingest` handle one message at a time, so order is fixed. The proposal sets it to 256, so messages run in parallel and a retried message can finish after a newer one. Device `freezer-07` sends every 30 seconds. The Timescale failover of 26 October runs from 10:14:03 to 10:14:40.

1. **10:14:03:** reading A (`read_at` 10:14:03, -20 °C) fails with `TransientError`. The handler naks it for 2 s.
2. **Deliveries 2 to 5 of A:** at 10:14:05, 10:14:09, 10:14:17 and 10:14:33 all fail. Delivery 5 naks for 32 s, so delivery 6 is due at 10:15:05.
3. **10:14:33:** reading B (-12 °C) is delivered in parallel. It fails twice, then succeeds at 10:14:47. `latest_reading` now holds B.
4. **10:15:03 and 10:15:05:** reading C saves normally at 10:15:03. Delivery 6 of A succeeds at 10:15:05. `UPSERT_LATEST` has no `read_at` check, so it writes A over C.
5. **10:15:05 to 10:15:33:** the live map and `freezer-temp-high` see -20 °C for `freezer-07`. The real value is above -15 °C. The next reading at 10:15:33 corrects it. For those 30 seconds the sensor looks healthy, and the alert's 10-minute window can restart.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 4.

4. **10:15:05:** delivery 6 of A inserts its row into `readings`. The upsert has `WHERE latest_reading.read_at < EXCLUDED.read_at`, so it changes nothing. `latest_reading` keeps C.
5. **10:15:33:** the map and the alert see the correct values throughout.

**What changes**

- `ingest/store.py`: `UPSERT_LATEST` gains `WHERE latest_reading.read_at < EXCLUDED.read_at` after `DO UPDATE SET ...`. `readings` is unaffected because reports order by `read_at`.
- `ingest/retry.py`: delete, as proposed.
- `ingest/handler.py`: delete the `with_retries` call. On `TransientError`, call `msg.nak(delay=...)`. At `num_delivered == 6`, publish to `ingest.dlq` and ack. The proposal does not cover other exceptions. Aelita suggests sending them to `ingest.dlq` on the first delivery, as today.
- `deploy/consumer.yaml`: `max_ack_pending: 1` becomes `256`, and `max_deliver: 1` becomes `6`.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends approval with the guard. The goal holds up. On 26 October, 17 stalls each held the consumer for 62 s. The failover pushed lag to 41,200 messages, and it took until 10:31:10 to clear. The retry schedule stays the same: 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 seconds, and 6 deliveries. The guard costs one `WHERE` clause. Two things can break:

- **Duplicates:** `readings` could get duplicate rows. `INSERT_HISTORY` has no `ON CONFLICT`, and I found no schema file in the repo, so I could not check for a unique key. A transient failure rolls back the transaction, so it leaves no row. Only a crash that outlives the 90 s `ack_wait` would redeliver a saved message.
- **Lost messages:** a crash during delivery 6 loses the message, because JetStream stops redelivering after `max_deliver`.

**Alternative: keep the loop, split consumers by device.** Each consumer takes a slice of `readings.<device_id>` and keeps `max_ack_pending: 1`. Order holds per device and needs no guard. It costs several consumers and a deploy change, and one stalled slice still blocks its own devices. It becomes the primary if `latest_reading` cannot take the `read_at` guard.
