Backoff alone will not fix the sync, because Juniper admits only 100 requests per minute and the job needs 468 minutes of that. Aelita recommends switching `sync/crm_client.py` to `POST /v2/contacts:batchUpsert`, which takes 100 contacts per request. It keeps a small `Retry-After` retry as a guard.

**Timeline without the fix**

1. Marketing imports 45,600 Fairhaven leads on 26 October. `changed_contacts` returns 46,800 rows at 01:00 UTC on 27 October.
2. `run_sync` starts 8 workers. Each one calls `upsert_contact`, which sends one PUT per contact.
3. The workers send about 400 requests per minute. Juniper allows 100 per minute per `JUNIPER_API_KEY`, so about 300 return 429.
4. `raise_for_status` raises. `sync_one` logs a warning and skips `mark_synced`. Only about 100 contacts per minute succeed.
5. With backoff added, the workers wait and retry, but Juniper still admits 100 requests per minute. The 7200 s deadline caps the job at 12,000 contacts. The log shows 11,940.
6. The job hits the deadline with 34,860 contacts pending. Pending Fairhaven leads miss the Friday 09:00 UTC campaign start in `TS-77`.

**Timeline with the fix**, diverges at step 2.

2. `run_sync` splits the 46,800 contacts into 468 chunks of 100. Each worker sends one `batchUpsert` call per chunk.
3. A call takes 5 to 7 seconds. Eight workers make at most about 80 calls per minute, under the limit of 100.
4. All 468 calls finish in about 6 minutes with no 429.
5. `run_sync` calls `mark_synced` for each contact that the response lists as succeeded.
6. The job ends minutes after 01:00 UTC. The Fairhaven leads reach Juniper before Friday. Each November import of 40,000 needs 400 calls, about 5 minutes.

**What changes**

- `sync/crm_client.py` adds `batch_upsert_contacts(contacts)`. It posts to `contacts:batchUpsert` and returns the per-contact results. On a 429 it sleeps for `Retry-After` and retries a few times. `upsert_contact` is no longer used by the job.
- `sync/run_sync.py` makes `sync_one` take a chunk of 100 instead of a single contact. It calls `mark_synced` only for contacts that succeeded. Failed ones stay unsynced for the next night.
- `deploy/cron.yaml` changes the image tag from `2026.10.3` to the new release.

**Recommendation.** Aelita recommends batching. It removes the cause, which is one request per contact against a limit of 100 requests per minute. It needs no contract change and finishes in minutes. The `Retry-After` guard costs about ten lines. The main risk is the response format. `docs/juniper-api.md` says the response lists a result per contact but gives no field names. The code needs a sample response to read success and failure correctly. The release must also ship before 01:00 UTC on 29 October to clear the backlog before Friday.

**Why not backoff only.** Backoff cuts the 429 noise but not the throughput. At 100 contacts per minute, 46,800 contacts take 7.8 hours against a 2-hour deadline. Raising the deadline does not help for the two November imports, which also need about 8 hours each.

**Alternative: enterprise tier.** Juniper allows 1,000 requests per minute, so per-contact PUTs finish in about 47 minutes. It costs 1,450 USD per month, and the 3-week lead time misses Friday. It becomes the primary only if batch results turn out to be unusable.
